#                                                                                Electromagnetic Spectrum 

## 1. Introduction
The electromagnetic (EM) spectrum is the range of all types of electromagnetic waves, arranged according to their frequency and wavelength. These waves are formed by vibrating electric and magnetic fields and do not need a medium to travel.

Electromagnetic waves travel at the speed of light **(3 × 10⁸ m/s)** in a vacuum.

The EM spectrum includes:
- Radio Waves
- Microwaves
- Infrared
- Visible Light
- Ultraviolet
- X-rays
- Gamma Rays

---

# 3. Classification of Electromagnetic Spectrum

---

## 3.1 Radio Waves
**Wavelength:** 1 mm – 100 km  
**Frequency:** 3 Hz – 300 GHz  

### Sources
- Radio/TV transmitters
- Cell towers
- Cosmic radiation

### Applications
- Radio broadcasting
- Television
- Satellite communication
- GPS
- Wi-Fi, Bluetooth

---

##  3.2 Microwaves
**Wavelength:** 1 mm – 1 m  
**Frequency:** 300 MHz – 300 GHz  

### Sources
- Magnetron in microwave ovens
- Radar equipment

### Applications
- Microwave ovens
- Radar systems
- Weather forecasting
- Satellite communication
- Wireless communication (Wi-Fi, 5G)

---

## 3.3 Infrared (IR) Radiation
**Wavelength:** 700 nm – 1 mm  

### Sources
- Sun  
- Human body (heat)  
- Remote control LEDs  

### Applications
- Remote controls
- Thermal imaging
- Night vision
- Optical fiber communication
- Medical heat treatments

---

## 3.4 Visible Light
**Wavelength:** 400 nm – 700 nm  
This is the only region visible to human eyes.

**Order of colors (increasing frequency):**  
Red → Orange → Yellow → Green → Blue → Indigo → Violet

### Applications
- Vision  
- Photography  
- Lasers  
- Optical communication  

---

## 3.5 Ultraviolet (UV) Radiation
**Wavelength:** 10 nm – 400 nm  

### Sources
- Sun  
- UV lamps  

### Applications
- Sterilization
- Fluorescent lamps
- DNA analysis
- Detecting fake currency

### Hazards
- Skin burns  
- Skin cancer  
- Eye damage

## 3.6 X-Rays
**Wavelength:** 0.01 nm – 10 nm  

### Sources
- X-ray tubes  
- Cosmic rays  

### Applications
- Medical imaging  
- Security scanning  
- Crystallography  

### Hazards
- Tissue damage  
- Requires lead shielding  

---

## 3.7 Gamma Rays
**Wavelength:** < 0.01 nm  
**Frequency:** Highest in the EM spectrum  

### Sources
- Nuclear reactions  
- Radioactive decay  
- Cosmic explosions  

### Applications
- Cancer radiotherapy  
- Sterilizing instruments  
- Studying atomic structures  

### Hazards
- Causes severe cell damage  
- Can be fatal at high exposures

# 4. Comparison Table

|---------------|------------------|------------------|------------------|
| Radio Waves   | > 1 m            | < 300 MHz        | Radio, TV, GPS   |
| Microwaves    | 1 mm – 1 m       | 300 MHz – 300 GHz | Radar, Wi-Fi     |
| Infrared      | 700 nm – 1 mm    | 300 GHz – 400 THz | Thermal imaging  |
| Visible Light | 400 – 700 nm     | 430 THz – 790 THz | Vision, lasers   |

# 7. Safety Precautions
Some EM waves (UV, X-rays, Gamma rays) are harmful.

### Prevention methods:
- Use sunscreen for UV protection  
- Lead shielding for X-ray exposure  
- Limit exposure time  
- Follow radiation safety guidelines
